Where does a general contractor's margin actually go?

Mostly into three places: bids that were never going to close, trades booked out of order, and money scattered across draws, sub invoices and HST that nobody reconciles until the project is over. The more trades on a job, the faster those leaks compound.

01

The bid you spent a weekend on

The caller said "an addition." You drove out, measured, chased three subs for pricing and spent Saturday on takeoffs. Then you found out there were no drawings, no budget, and the owner was "just getting numbers." Every tire-kicker bid costs the same hours as the one you win.

02

One failed inspection, five rebooked trades

The framing inspection gets pushed. The insulator is already booked for Thursday, drywall for Monday, and the HVAC sub moved another job to fit you in. Now you're rebooking everyone by text, and the plan that lived in your head no longer matches anyone's calendar.

03

Draws, subs and HST in five spreadsheets

Which draw went out? Did the electrician's invoice include HST? Is the holdback tracked anywhere? What is each sub still owed? The answers are scattered across a spreadsheet, an inbox and the bank statement. Nobody can tell you the job's real margin while there's still time to fix it.

How the AI Caller Works

Enquiry to final draw

What does a build look like on NexusCRM?

One system carries the project from the first phone call to the holdback release, with every trade, draw and dollar in the same place.

The AI Caller picks up at 6:22 p.m. and learns it's a 600 sq ft addition with drawings done and a real budget. The lead lands in NexusCRM with a summary and transcript, so the site walk goes on the calendar for a job worth bidding.

The signed snapshot locks with timestamp and IP. The invoice, with its deposit, draws and holdback milestones, is created automatically along with the project and the schedule entry. Not signed yet? Expiry reminders go out, and the AI Caller can follow up by phone.

Each trade gets its own visits. Insulation stays Blocked until the framing inspection passes, changes sync to Google Calendar, and the 6 PM day-before reminder makes sure the right sub shows up on the right day.

Changes and costs land as they happen

Change orders get priced and e-signed before the work starts. Sub invoices, material receipts and crew hours hit the live P&L the day they occur, and the at-risk watchlist flags an overrun while you can still act on it.

Final draw with a known margin

Each draw is tracked against the schedule, the holdback milestone is billed when it comes due, and every sub balance reconciles. You close the project knowing exactly what it made.

Can NexusCRM handle progress draws and holdback on a construction project?

Yes. Invoices carry payment schedule milestones, so a $143,000 addition can be billed as a deposit plus progress draws tied to stages such as foundation, framing, rough-ins, and substantial completion. Many contractors put the statutory holdback on its own milestone so it is tracked separately and billed when it comes due. Payments are recorded against each milestone, status moves from Pending to Partial to Paid automatically, receivables aging shows what is 30, 60, or 90+ days out, and HST is handled natively on every draw. NexusCRM tracks the money; confirm the holdback rules for your contract with your own lawyer or advisor.

How does NexusCRM keep subcontractors sequenced across trades?

Each trade's work is a scheduled visit or task on the project. The crew calendar shows month and week views coloured by crew lead with multi-day spans, and every change is pushed to multiple Google Calendar accounts by a background worker within about 60 seconds. Tasks can be marked Blocked with a required reason — waiting on framing inspection, waiting on HVAC rough-in — so nobody books insulation before the inspection passes. Everyone assigned to a visit gets an automatic 6 PM reminder the evening before, plus added and removed notifications, and field staff only see the jobs they are assigned to.

How do change orders work on a larger project?

Revise the quote, price the change, and send it for online acceptance. The owner or client approves on their phone with a typed signature, and that version is stored as an immutable signed snapshot with IP address and timestamp. The revision history shows exactly what changed between versions, invoice PDFs keep an immutable version history, and billing charges exactly what was accepted. On multi-phase work, each phase is its own project inside a project group, so a change on phase two lands on phase two's P&L instead of muddying the whole job.

How do I track what each subcontractor is owed, including HST?

The subcontractor directory shows one combined balance for each sub, whether you pay them through logged labour, their invoices, or both, with HST attributed per CRA gross-payment rules. Sub invoices and materials land on the job as categorized expenses with receipt uploads, labour is costed at pay rates locked when the crew was assigned, and recoverable HST is tracked separately, so the live per-job P&L shows real margin while the project is still open. Payouts can be marked paid one at a time or in bulk, with a CSV of everything just paid for your bookkeeper.

When the call ends, the lead lands in NexusCRM with a summary and transcript, tagged AI Caller, so you can decide which jobs are worth a site walk.
