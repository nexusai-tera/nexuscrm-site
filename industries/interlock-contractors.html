Sound familiar?

Where does an interlock job's margin disappear?

Under the surface, mostly. A base quoted too thin, a settling call you can't answer, a winter full of quotes nobody followed up. Customers see the pavers, but most of an interlock job's cost is in the dig-out, the base, and what happens after.

01

The base you under-priced

You quoted a driveway off your patio rate per square foot. A driveway needs a deeper dig and a lot more Granular A. Two extra loads, a second bin and an extra excavation day later, the job that looked like 38% on paper closes in the teens.

02

The settling call from two summers ago

A customer calls about a sunken section by the downspout. Is it under warranty? What base depth went in, which crew laid it, and are there photos of the compaction? The answers are on a former foreman's phone and a paper quote in a banker's box.

03

Winter quotes that never wake up

You quote sixty jobs between November and March. By April, nobody remembers who said "call me in spring," who wanted the premium slab, or which quotes still carry last year's paver prices. The customers you forget book whoever called them first.

Adding seasonal labourers in April never changes the bill. Annual prepay earns two months free, financed implementation is available, and the AI Caller is an optional add-on from $297/month.

Can NexusCRM price interlock driveways differently from patios and walkways?

Yes, because it prices from your own quotes rather than one generic rate. A driveway needs deeper excavation and more Granular A than a backyard patio, and your past driveway quotes already reflect that — so voice-to-quote drafts excavation and disposal, base, pavers, border course, and polymeric sand as separate lines using rates from similar jobs you have priced before. Any line under your historical low is flagged, and the AI Bid Advisor compares the draft's margin with your price sheets and up to 25 similar completed jobs before you send.

Can it produce crew job sheets with base, paver and poly sand quantities?

Yes. AI scope of work drafts the work, materials, timeline, and special notes from the job's paperwork, and warns you when its quantities drift from the ratios you use — so a base tonnage that looks light for a driveway gets caught before the aggregate order goes in. You confirm the scope side by side, then print a versioned PDF work order with a materials table and signature lines. Crews also see the scope, photos, and documents for their own jobs on their phones, with financials hidden.

How does NexusCRM handle winter quotes and the spring start rush?

Quote all winter, then start fast. Leads that say “call me in April” are parked with Pause until spring and a reason, so bulk sends skip them and they are not lost in a spreadsheet. When you are ready, a bulk promo campaign can send selected quotes a “book by March 31 and save 10%” offer with auto-expiring discounts, and every quote reminds the customer three days before and on the day it expires. Accepted quotes create the invoice and the project automatically and land in Awaiting Schedule, so the spring start list builds itself.

What about settling complaints and warranty calls on old interlock jobs?

Pull up the job and the answer is already there. The warranty terms were snapshot-copied onto the signed quote from your warranty library, so later template edits never change what that customer agreed to. Before, In Progress, and After photos are tagged on the project, the versioned crew job sheet shows the scope you sent the crew, and the activity timeline and audit log show who did what and when. Search by address or phone finds the customer in seconds, and the repair can be quoted, accepted, and scheduled like any other job.
