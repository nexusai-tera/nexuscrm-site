Where does a renovation's profit actually go?

It leaks — through unpriced change orders, homeowner hand-holding nobody bills for, and costs nobody tallies until tax season. Multi-week renos punish loose tracking harder than any service call ever will.

01

The margin you discover in March

The kitchen wrapped in October. Sub invoices trickled in through November. The lumber receipts lived in the truck. Your accountant tells you in March that the job made 9% — or lost money. By then there's nothing you can do about it.

02

Change orders that never got priced

"While you're in the ceiling, can you add three pot lights?" Said on site, done that afternoon, never written up, never signed. A dozen of those across a basement finish and your contingency is gone — and you can't prove any of it was extra.

03

The homeowner living in your job site

They're washing dishes in the bathtub while you redo the kitchen, so the texts come at 9 p.m.: is the countertop installer still coming Thursday? When's the next draw due? Every answer costs you an evening, and one missed reply turns a happy client into a stressed one.

AI in the field

What does renovation estimating and CRM in one look like?

It looks like walking a basement for two minutes, talking through the scope, and getting a priced draft quote — built from your own past quotes, with finish options and a draw schedule attached. No evening at the kitchen table with a calculator.

  • Voice-to-quote — record the site walk; NexusCRM transcribes it, matches the customer, and drafts priced line items
  • Grounded in your history — prices come from your own past quotes, not generic cost guides, and lines under your historical low get flagged
  • AI Bid Advisor — checks margin against your price sheets and up to 25 similar completed jobs before you send
  • Quote polish — one click rewrites line descriptions in your company voice, with a word-level diff preview

How the AI Caller Works

First call to final draw

What does a renovation look like on NexusCRM?

One system carries the job from the weekend enquiry to the final draw — with the margin known at every step.

The Saturday-night call gets answered

A homeowner calls about finishing their basement at 9:26 p.m. The AI Caller qualifies the room, scope, budget range, and timeline, and the lead lands in NexusCRM with a summary and transcript. A web-form lead from your Meta ad gets called within minutes instead of Monday.

Final draw with a known margin

Annual prepay earns two months free, financed implementation is available, and the AI Caller for inbound, outbound and follow-up calls is an optional add-on from $297/month.

Can homeowners choose between finish levels on one renovation quote?

Yes. Quotes are multi-option, so one kitchen quote can price builder-grade, mid-range, and premium finishes as Good, Better, and Best — cabinets, counters, tile, and fixtures as their own lines under each option. The homeowner opens the quote on their phone, ticks the options they want with a live running total, and signs; the acceptance is locked as an immutable snapshot with timestamp and IP, and billing charges exactly what was accepted. The warranty you chose is snapshot-copied onto the quote, so later template edits never change what they signed.

Can NexusCRM handle deposits and progress draws on a renovation?

Yes. Invoices carry payment schedules, so a $55,000 basement gets a deposit plus draws tied to milestones — rough-in, drywall, completion — instead of one invoice at the end. Reusable milestone templates carry from the quote to the invoice automatically, every payment is recorded against the schedule, the homeowner sees paid and due status in their portal, and the job P&L reconciles what has been collected against what is still owed. HST is handled natively on every draw.

How does NexusCRM handle change orders on a renovation?

When the homeowner asks for three more pot lights or a bigger island, you revise the quote, price the change, and send it for one-tap online acceptance from their phone. The accepted version is locked as an immutable signed snapshot with timestamp and IP, and the full revision history shows exactly what changed and when. Scope creep gets priced and signed instead of quietly absorbed.

How does NexusCRM cut down on homeowners calling to ask about their job?

Every homeowner gets a private customer portal link — no login — showing project status, tasks, photos, documents, the quote, invoices, and a milestone timeline. Automatic branded emails go out when the job is scheduled, rescheduled, started, and completed, and a notification audit trail shows exactly what was sent and when. Strict field whitelists mean internal costs and margins structurally cannot reach the portal, so you can share progress photos without sharing your numbers.
