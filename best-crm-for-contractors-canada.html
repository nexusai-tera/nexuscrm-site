Methodology

How did we rank the best contractor CRMs?

We ranked all six platforms on four things that actually decide whether the software pays for itself: realistic monthly cost for a 6-person team, converted to CAD; job-costing depth (can you see a live per-job P&L, or just a revenue number?); how much AI is genuinely included versus sold as an add-on; and Canadian fit — CAD pricing, HST handling, and data residency. Feature checklists came last, because every vendor's checklist looks identical until you price it for a real crew.

#2 · Best self-serve

2. Jobber — best self-serve contractor CRM

PRICING: CORE $49/MO (MONTH-TO-MONTH) · GROW $199–$499/MO · +$29 PER ADDITIONAL USER

Jobber is the best pick if you want to sign up tonight and send your first quote tomorrow — no sales call, no implementation, no contract. It's the most widely used option among small residential contractors in North America for a reason, and as a fellow Canadian company (Edmonton, AB) it handles the local basics well.

It also ships two things today that NexusCRM has only on its roadmap: two-way SMS and online card payments. If your workflow depends on texting customers and taking cards in-app this quarter, Jobber is genuinely ahead of us on those two features. Where it thins out is the back half of the job: job costing is basic and reserved for higher tiers, there's no payroll or commission tracking, and the per-user meter means a growing team creeps from $199 toward $499-plus.

Pros

  • True self-serve — running in an afternoon, 14-day free trial, $49 Core is month-to-month
  • SMS and online payments ship today — still roadmap items for NexusCRM
  • Polished mobile apps and a large integration ecosystem, refined over a decade
  • Copilot AI is free while in beta
  • Canadian company — Edmonton HQ, strong Canadian support base

Cons

  • Per-user fees — +$29/user pushes a growing 6-person team well past the base price
  • Job costing is basic and gated to higher tiers — no live per-job P&L with crew pay models
  • No payroll or commissions — you'll still reconcile crew pay elsewhere
  • Shared multi-tenant SaaS — no private instance or Canadian data-residency option
Bottom lineSolo to ~5 people doing straightforward service work: Jobber is an excellent, low-risk choice. Project-based crews who need real margins tend to outgrow it — that's the gap NexusCRM was built in.

#3 · Best consumer polish

3. Housecall Pro — best homeowner-facing experience

PRICING: BASIC $79/MO · MAX $329/MO USD · +$35 PER ADDITIONAL USER

Housecall Pro is the best choice when the homeowner's experience closes the deal — its booking flow, on-my-way texts, review automation, and built-in consumer financing are the slickest in this list. Pros

  • Consumer financing built in — genuinely helps close large residential tickets
  • Best-in-class homeowner experience — booking, notifications, review requests
  • SMS and online payments today, plus free advisory AI on all plans
  • Easy start — $79 Basic plan and quick self-serve onboarding

Cons

  • Per-user fees — +$35/user on top of $329 MAX adds up fast for crews
  • Light job costing — fine for service calls, thin for multi-week projects
  • Call-answering AI is a paid add-on; no payroll beyond timesheets
  • US vendor — USD pricing, US-first integrations, no Canadian data residency
Bottom lineResidential service businesses selling big-ticket jobs to homeowners — HVAC replacements, reno packages — get real value from the polish and financing. Crews that live and die on job margins will want more depth.

#4 · Best enterprise

4. ServiceTitan — best for enterprise-scale shops

PRICING: $245–$500 PER TECHNICIAN/MO USD · $5K–$15K IMPLEMENTATION · QUOTE-ONLY · 12-MO CONTRACT

ServiceTitan is the best platform on this list for shops with 15+ technicians — nothing else matches its dispatch board, call-centre integration, marketing attribution, and reporting depth. It's the system large residential HVAC, plumbing, and electrical companies (and the private-equity consolidators buying them) standardize on, and at that scale it earns its price.

Below that scale, the economics stop working. Pricing is per technician — $245–$500 USD each per month — so a 6-tech shop is looking at $1,470–$3,000 USD monthly (roughly $2,000–$4,000+ CAD), plus a $5,000–$15,000 implementation, a 12-month contract, quote-only pricing, and AI sold as paid Pro add-ons. Verified user reports consistently describe onboarding measured in months, not weeks.

Pros

  • Deepest enterprise feature set in the trade — dispatching, CSR/phone integration, inventory, marketing ROI
  • Genuinely deep job costing and payroll modules at enterprise grade
  • Scales to hundreds of technicians across multiple locations
  • Serious reporting — the analytics large operators actually need

Cons

  • Per-technician pricing — $1,470–$3,000 USD/mo for a 6-tech shop before add-ons
  • $5K–$15K implementation, quote-only pricing, 12-month contract
  • AI features are paid Pro add-ons, not included
  • Months-long onboarding per verified user reports — overkill below ~10–15 techs
Bottom lineIf you're running 15+ techs with a dispatch desk, ServiceTitan is the grown-up answer and worth the quote. If you're a 6-person crew, you'd be paying enterprise prices for depth you can get flat-rate elsewhere.

Decision guide

How do you choose the right contractor CRM?

Choose by team size first and trade second: solo operators should buy cheap and self-serve, crews of 3–20 should buy job-costing depth, and 15+ tech shops should buy enterprise dispatch. Here's the honest version.

Solo or two-person shop

Markate (~$69–$129 USD all-in) or Jobber Core ($49, month-to-month). Honestly, a disciplined spreadsheet and calendar still work at this scale — buy software when quote volume, not ambition, demands it.

2–5 people, simple service work

Jobber Grow if you want self-serve setup and need SMS and card payments today. NexusCRM Core ($295 flat CAD, unlimited users) if missed follow-ups and unknown margins are the actual problem — see pricing.

3–20 crew, quote- and project-based work

This is NexusCRM Pro's home turf: flat $425 CAD, live per-job P&L, payroll, and every AI feature included. General contractors, landscapers and hardscapers, plumbers running installs — start with the contractor edition.

15+ technicians, dispatch-heavy

ServiceTitan. Budget $2,000–$4,000+ CAD monthly, a five-figure implementation, and months of onboarding — at that scale, its dispatch and reporting depth is worth it.

By trade: project-based trades (general contracting, landscaping, hardscape, renovation) should weight job costing and payroll heaviest — that points to NexusCRM or Contractor Foreman. High-volume residential service (plumbing service calls, HVAC maintenance) should weight speed and homeowner communication — today that favours Jobber or Housecall Pro for SMS, with NexusCRM's voice-to-quote and automation as the counterweight, and SMS on our roadmap stated plainly.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

What CRM do most contractors use?

By install base, Jobber is the most common choice for small residential contractors in North America, and ServiceTitan dominates large home-service companies — but the honest answer is that most small contractors still run on spreadsheets, texts, and memory. Popularity isn't fit: Jobber's per-user fees and basic job costing suit simple service work, while project-based crews tend to outgrow it.

How much should a contractor CRM cost?

For a 6-person Canadian team in 2026, expect roughly $95–$680 CAD a month for SMB tools like Markate, Jobber, or Housecall Pro once user fees and exchange rates are counted, $425 CAD flat for NexusCRM Pro, and $2,000–$4,000+ CAD for ServiceTitan. A sane benchmark: if the software doesn't clearly save more admin time and win more quotes than it costs, it's mispriced for you.

Do I need field service software or a CRM?

If you schedule crews and invoice for completed work, you need field service management — which includes a CRM — not a generic sales CRM. Tools like HubSpot, Salesforce, or Pipedrive track contacts and deals but have no job costing, crew scheduling, or quote-to-invoice flow. Every platform on this list is field-service software; the 'CRM' label is just how contractors search for it.

What's the best free option for contractors?

There's no genuinely good free contractor CRM. Jobber's 14-day free trial is the best way to test-drive one, and at solo scale a disciplined spreadsheet plus a calendar honestly works until quote volume grows. NexusCRM has no free tier — it's an implemented platform with a 12-month initial term, which only makes sense once admin hours are visibly costing you jobs.

Which contractor CRM is best for Canadian data residency?

NexusCRM is the only platform on this list offering a private, dedicated instance per client with Canadian hosting available, which supports PIPEDA-aligned data-residency requirements. Jobber is a Canadian company but runs shared multi-tenant SaaS, and Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Contractor Foreman, and Markate are US vendors storing data in US cloud infrastructure.

How long does switching contractor CRMs take?

Self-serve tools like Jobber or Markate take a few days to a few weeks of your own evenings to set up and migrate. NexusCRM is implemented for you — most businesses are live in about two weeks, including data migration, branding, and team training. ServiceTitan onboarding typically runs months, per verified user reports. The real cost is usually re-keying data, which is why implementation-included platforms exist.
