The procurement question

Why does Canadian data residency matter for a CRM?

Canadian data residency matters because it has become a de facto procurement requirement for many Canadian buyers — even though PIPEDA itself does not mandate storing data in Canada. Three forces drive the question onto vendor questionnaires:

§

PIPEDA: accountability, not geography

PIPEDA makes your business accountable for personal information you hand to any processor, anywhere. Cross-border transfers are legal with comparable contractual protection — but the accountability never transfers. A shorter, domestic chain of custody is simply easier to stand behind.

⚜

Quebec Law 25 raises the bar

Before communicating personal information outside Quebec, organizations must complete a privacy impact assessment covering the destination's protections. Canadian hosting doesn't remove that analysis — but it materially simplifies it.

⚑

US CLOUD Act exposure

The CLOUD Act lets US authorities compel US-based providers to disclose data under their control, regardless of where the servers sit. "Hosted in Canada by a US company" therefore doesn't fully answer the question a careful procurement team is asking.

Straight talk: any vendor claiming PIPEDA requires Canadian hosting is overselling. What's true is that municipalities, school boards, property managers, and health-adjacent organizations increasingly write residency into vendor requirements — and "yes, in Canada, on our own isolated database" ends that conversation quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Is NexusCRM PIPEDA compliant?

PIPEDA has no official certification program, so no vendor can honestly hand you a "PIPEDA certified" badge — be wary of any that do. What NexusCRM provides is PIPEDA-aligned practices that support your obligations as the accountable organization: a dedicated instance with an isolated database, four-role access control, an append-only audit log on every change, Canadian hosting available, and full data export any time.

Does PIPEDA require my data to be stored in Canada?

No. PIPEDA is accountability-based: you may transfer personal information to a processor in another country, but you remain responsible for it and must ensure comparable protection through contract. In practice, though, Canadian residency has become a de facto requirement in many procurement processes, and Quebec's Law 25 adds a formal assessment before personal information leaves Quebec.

Where is my NexusCRM data hosted?

On your own private, dedicated instance with its own isolated database — never pooled with other companies. Canadian hosting is available and is chosen during implementation, so if your clients, partners, or procurement policies require data in Canada, that is how your instance gets provisioned.

Who can see my data?

Your team, according to the four roles you assign — Owner, Rep, Crew Lead, and Crew — with an append-only audit log recording every change, human or AI. Because your instance has its own database, there is no shared pool where a bug or misconfiguration could expose your records to another company. NexusAI GTA staff access is limited to support and maintenance.

Is NexusCRM pricing in Canadian dollars?

Yes. Core is $295/month, Pro is $425/month, and Elite is $795/month — all flat CAD with unlimited users, published on the pricing page. There is no exchange-rate creep: US tools priced in USD cost roughly 35% more in CAD at 2026 rates, and that gap moves with the currency market, not with your budget.

Does NexusCRM handle HST?

Yes, natively. Invoices calculate HST automatically, job profitability tracks recoverable HST separately from cost, and crew and subcontractor ledgers are kept CRA-sensible for year-end. HST is not a bolted-on tax field — it flows through quotes, invoices, expenses, and P&L consistently.

What about Quebec Law 25?

Law 25 requires organizations to assess privacy impacts before communicating personal information outside Quebec. Hosting your CRM in Canada simplifies that analysis considerably compared with a US-hosted tool. NexusCRM supports your Law 25 obligations with residency options, access controls, and audit logs — but talk to your privacy counsel for advice specific to your business.
