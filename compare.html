Head to head

NexusCRM vs Jobber

Where Jobber is strong: Jobber is probably the most polished self-serve field service app on the market — and it's Canadian too, built in Edmonton. Core starts at just $49/month, the mobile app is excellent, onboarding is genuinely easy, and it ships two-way SMS and online payments today. For a solo operator or a two-person crew doing straightforward repeat work, it's a very fair choice.

Where NexusCRM wins: the meter and the depth. Jobber Grow runs $199–$499/month plus $29 for every additional user, so a growing crew watches the bill climb with every hire — while NexusCRM Pro stays a flat $425 with unlimited users. And Jobber's AI and automations arrive partially, on higher tiers; NexusCRM includes all of it: voice-to-quote from a recorded site walk priced from your own historical quotes, an AI Bid Advisor that checks margin against your price sheets and up to 25 similar completed jobs, live per-job P&L with four crew pay models, and built-in payroll and commissions — plus a dedicated private instance instead of a seat on shared SaaS.

Who should pick which: pick Jobber if you want $49/month self-serve simplicity and need texting today. Pick NexusCRM if you're a 4–15 person crew quoting complex jobs, you care about real margins per job, and you're tired of paying per seat. See how the workflow runs end-to-end on the features page.

Bottom lineAt 6+ users with quoted, margin-sensitive work, NexusCRM is cheaper, deeper, and includes the AI Jobber charges tiers for. Below 3 users with simple repeat jobs, Jobber's self-serve entry price is hard to argue with.

Head to head

NexusCRM vs Housecall Pro

Where Housecall Pro is strong: it's a slick, consumer-style app for on-demand residential service — dispatch, "on my way" texts, online booking, and card payments all work well today. Basic starts at $79/month, and for a one-truck HVAC, plumbing, or appliance-repair operation running quick service calls, that polish counts for a lot.

Where NexusCRM wins: anywhere the job is quoted rather than dispatched. Housecall Pro MAX runs $329/month plus $35 per additional user, its AI is advisory (with call AI paid extra), and its job costing is limited. NexusCRM turns a 90-second recorded site walk into a priced draft quote grounded in your own quoting history, tracks live per-job profitability down to photographed receipts (read by Claude-vision AI), and handles crew payroll and rep commissions natively — at a flat price that doesn't grow with headcount.

Who should pick which: pick Housecall Pro if you run high-volume residential service calls and need customer texting and in-app payments this quarter — both are still on NexusCRM's roadmap, honestly. Pick NexusCRM if you quote projects, chase margin, and want the office work to do itself — see how contractors run on NexusCRM.

Bottom lineHousecall Pro is a dispatch tool with payments; NexusCRM is a quote-to-profit operations platform. Match the tool to which of those describes your week.

NexusCRM vs ServiceTitan

Where ServiceTitan is strong: for large operations it's the category heavyweight, and deservedly so — enterprise-grade dispatch boards, call booking, marketing attribution, and reporting that a 50-technician shop genuinely needs. Its job costing is deep. If you run a call centre and a fleet, it belongs on your shortlist.

Where NexusCRM wins: everywhere below enterprise scale. ServiceTitan is quote-only, runs $245–$500 per technician per month, and implementations reportedly cost $5,000–$15,000 and take months. A 6-tech shop is looking at roughly $1,470–$3,000 every month for a system built — and priced — for someone three times their size. NexusCRM Pro is $425 flat for the whole company, implementation is $5,500 (financing available), you're live in two weeks, and every AI feature is included rather than sold as an add-on. You also get something ServiceTitan can't offer: a dedicated private instance with your own database, hosted in Canada if you need it.

Who should pick which: at 15+ technicians with dedicated office staff, ServiceTitan's scale features may justify its cost. From 2 to 20 people, NexusCRM delivers the parts that actually move profit — quoting speed, live job P&L, payroll — at under a third of the price. Run your own numbers against the published pricing.

Bottom lineFor a small business, ServiceTitan means paying enterprise prices to use 20% of an enterprise product. NexusCRM is that 20% — the profitable part — built for your size, at a price published right on the site.

NexusCRM vs GoHighLevel

Where GoHighLevel is strong: marketing. Funnels, review requests, missed-call text-back, nurture sequences — as a white-label platform agencies resell at $297–$497/month (plus a $97/month AI add-on), it's a legitimately capable lead-generation machine, and a good agency can configure it well.

Where NexusCRM wins: everything that happens after the lead. GoHighLevel has no job costing, no crew scheduling, no payroll, no field operations — it hands a lead to the same spreadsheet chaos you already have. NexusCRM takes the lead through quote, one-tap online acceptance, automatic invoice and project creation, crew scheduling with Google Calendar sync, and a live P&L — with zero double entry. There's also a structural difference: with an agency-resold GHL account, your data sits in the agency's multi-tenant setup and your relationship is with a middleman. NexusCRM gives you a dedicated private instance, directly from the company that builds the product in Toronto.

Who should pick which: if your only problem is lead flow and follow-up, a GHL agency can help. If your problem is running the business — quoting, scheduling, margins, payroll — that's what NexusCRM was built for, inside a real GTA contracting business. Many clients keep their marketing agency and pipe the leads straight into NexusCRM.

Bottom lineGoHighLevel markets your business; NexusCRM runs it. They're different categories wearing the same "CRM" label.

Comparison questions, answered

Is there a cheaper alternative to ServiceTitan in Canada?

Yes. ServiceTitan runs $245–$500 per technician per month plus a $5,000–$15,000 implementation, per public pricing pages and verified user reports — roughly $1,470–$3,000/month for a 6-tech shop. NexusCRM Pro is a flat $425/month CAD with unlimited users and a $5,500 one-time implementation (financing available), keeps deep job costing, and includes all AI. It's built and supported in Toronto, with Canadian hosting available.

What is the best Jobber alternative for a growing team?

If the per-user meter is the pain, NexusCRM is the strongest Jobber alternative: Jobber Grow runs $199–$499/month plus $29 per additional user, so the bill climbs with every hire, while NexusCRM Pro stays a flat $425/month with unlimited users. It also adds things Jobber doesn't have — voice-to-quote AI priced from your own historical quotes, live per-job P&L with four crew pay models, and built-in payroll and commissions. If you're a solo operator who wants $49/month self-serve, Jobber Core remains a fair choice.

Does NexusCRM have SMS and online payments like Jobber and Housecall Pro?

Not yet — honest answer. SMS text messaging, online card payments, and QuickBooks Online sync are the headline items on the current roadmap. Today NexusCRM covers email automation with open and click tracking, one-tap online quote acceptance with immutable signed snapshots, payment schedules on invoices, and CSV exports for your bookkeeper. If your workflow depends on two-way texting or in-app card payments this quarter, Jobber and Housecall Pro are genuinely better on those two features right now.

How is NexusCRM different from a GoHighLevel agency CRM?

GoHighLevel is a white-label marketing platform that agencies resell at $297–$497/month plus a $97/month AI add-on. It's strong at funnels, review requests, and follow-up sequences, but it has no job costing, no crew scheduling, no payroll, and no field operations — and your data lives in the reselling agency's multi-tenant account. NexusCRM is an operations platform built inside a real GTA contracting business, running quote-to-invoice-to-schedule-to-P&L on a dedicated private instance you get directly from the product company.

Can NexusCRM migrate my data from Jobber or Housecall Pro?

Yes — migration is part of every NexusCRM implementation. We import your customers, price book, and open work, apply your branding to quotes, invoices, and the customer portal, connect Google Calendar and your lead sources, and train your team. Most businesses are live in about two weeks, not two months, and Pro-tier implementations include white-glove migration.

Why do per-user fees matter in field service software?

Because they tax growth and punish adoption. At $29–$35 per user, a 6-person team pays an extra $174–$210 every month on top of the base plan — and in practice teams respond by sharing logins, which destroys accountability and audit trails. NexusCRM's flat pricing with unlimited users means every crew member, rep, and bookkeeper gets their own role-scoped account, with every change logged with before/after history.
